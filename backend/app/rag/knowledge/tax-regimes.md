## The two regimes
India has two personal income-tax regimes. The **new regime** is the default: if you do nothing, your employer and the tax department apply it. It has lower slab rates but removes most deductions and exemptions. The **old regime** has higher rates but lets you claim deductions such as Section 80C, 80D, HRA and home-loan interest. Salaried people can choose each year when filing their return.

## New regime slabs (FY 2025-26)
- Up to ₹4 lakh: nil
- ₹4–8 lakh: 5%
- ₹8–12 lakh: 10%
- ₹12–16 lakh: 15%
- ₹16–20 lakh: 20%
- ₹20–24 lakh: 25%
- Above ₹24 lakh: 30%

A rebate under Section 87A means no tax is payable on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh. Salaried taxpayers also get a standard deduction of ₹75,000, so salary income up to about ₹12.75 lakh is effectively tax-free.

## Old regime slabs (below 60 years)
- Up to ₹2.5 lakh: nil
- ₹2.5–5 lakh: 5%
- ₹5–10 lakh: 20%
- Above ₹10 lakh: 30%

The 87A rebate makes taxable income up to ₹5 lakh tax-free. The standard deduction for salaried people is ₹50,000. A 4% health and education cess applies on top of tax in both regimes.

## How to choose
Add up the deductions you would genuinely claim under the old regime (80C, 80D, HRA, home-loan interest, NPS 80CCD(1B)). If those are small, the new regime usually wins. People with large HRA claims and a home loan sometimes still save under the old regime. Compare both each year — most tax portals and the income-tax e-filing site show both calculations.

## Important
Slabs and limits change in Union Budgets. Always confirm the current year's figures on incometax.gov.in or with a tax professional before filing.
