## Credit-card interest
Unpaid credit-card balances typically cost about 36–48% a year, which is far more than any investment returns. Paying only the "minimum amount due" keeps you in debt for a long time, and interest is charged on the full outstanding amount. Always try to pay the total due by the due date.

## Avalanche vs snowball
- **Avalanche:** pay minimums on everything, then put every extra rupee towards the highest-interest debt first. This saves the most money.
- **Snowball:** clear the smallest balance first for quick wins and motivation.
Either works if you stick with it; avalanche is cheaper.

## EMIs
A common guideline is to keep total EMIs below about 30–40% of take-home pay. Pre-paying a high-interest loan (personal loan, card balance) early usually gives a better "return" than investing. Home loans are cheaper and may have tax benefits in the old regime.

## Balance transfers and personal loans
Converting card debt to a lower-rate personal loan or EMI can reduce interest, but check processing fees and avoid running up the card again.

## Buy now, pay later
BNPL and no-cost EMI offers are still credit; missed payments hurt your CIBIL score.
