## EPF (Employees' Provident Fund)
For most salaried employees, 12% of basic salary plus DA goes to EPF, with a matching employer contribution (part of which goes to the pension scheme, EPS). The interest rate is declared each year (8.25% for FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25). Your contribution counts under 80C in the old regime. Withdrawals before five years of continuous service can be taxable.

## PPF (Public Provident Fund)
- Government-backed, available at post offices and banks.
- Deposit between ₹500 and ₹1.5 lakh per financial year.
- 15-year lock-in, extendable in blocks of 5 years; partial withdrawals allowed from the 7th year.
- Interest is set by the government every quarter (it has been 7.1% for several quarters — check the current rate).
- EEE status: contributions (under 80C, old regime), interest and maturity are tax-free.

## NPS (National Pension System)
- Tier I is the retirement account: money is locked until age 60, with limited partial withdrawals.
- At 60, up to 60% can be withdrawn tax-free; at least 40% must buy an annuity (a pension).
- You choose the equity/debt mix (active choice) or a life-cycle fund that reduces equity as you age.
- Tax benefits: 80CCD(1B) extra ₹50,000 (old regime) and employer contributions under 80CCD(2) (both regimes).
- Tier II is a flexible account without a lock-in and without tax benefits for most people.

## Which to use
EPF is often automatic. PPF suits safe, long-term, tax-free saving. NPS adds market-linked growth with a strict lock-in. Many people use a mix. Starting early matters more than picking the perfect product, because compounding needs time.
