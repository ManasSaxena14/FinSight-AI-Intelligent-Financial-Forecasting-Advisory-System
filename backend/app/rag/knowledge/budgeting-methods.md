## 50/30/20
Roughly 50% of take-home pay for needs (rent, groceries, bills, EMIs), 30% for wants (eating out, shopping, entertainment, travel) and 20% for savings and investments. In expensive cities rent alone can exceed 30%, so treat the split as a starting point, not a rule.

## Pay yourself first
Move your savings out on salary day — an auto-transfer to a savings account or an automatic SIP — and spend what is left. This works better than saving whatever remains at month end.

## Zero-based budgeting
Give every rupee a job: income minus fixed costs, minus variable categories, minus savings, until nothing is left unassigned. It takes more effort but shows exactly where money goes.

## Envelope / category caps
Set a monthly cap per category (for example food delivery or shopping) and check it weekly. FinSight's suggested budgets and month-to-date tracking do this automatically.

## Weekly check-ins
A 10-minute weekly review catches problems early: compare spending so far with the month's budget, look at large or unusual transactions, and adjust the rest of the month.

## Common leaks in India
Food-delivery fees and small orders, unused OTT and app subscriptions on UPI autopay, impulse online shopping during sales, and cab surge pricing.
