## Section 80C — up to ₹1.5 lakh (old regime only)
A combined limit of ₹1,50,000 per financial year across: EPF employee contribution, PPF, ELSS mutual funds, life-insurance premiums, 5-year tax-saving fixed deposits, National Savings Certificate (NSC), Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana, children's tuition fees and the principal part of a home-loan EMI. Many salaried people already use a large part of this through EPF, so check your payslip before investing more just for tax.

## Section 80CCD(1B) — extra ₹50,000 for NPS (old regime)
Contributions to NPS Tier I get an additional deduction of up to ₹50,000, over and above the ₹1.5 lakh 80C limit.

## Section 80CCD(2) — employer's NPS contribution
Your employer's contribution to your NPS account is deductible (up to 14% of basic salary plus DA) and is one of the few deductions also allowed in the new regime.

## Section 80D — health insurance (old regime)
- Self, spouse and children: up to ₹25,000 (₹50,000 if you are a senior citizen)
- Parents: an additional ₹25,000 (₹50,000 if they are senior citizens)
- Preventive health check-ups: up to ₹5,000, included within these limits

## House Rent Allowance (HRA) — old regime
If your salary includes HRA and you pay rent, part of HRA is exempt. The exempt amount is the lowest of: actual HRA received; rent paid minus 10% of basic salary; and 50% of basic (metro cities) or 40% (non-metro).

## Home loan
Old regime: interest on a self-occupied home loan is deductible up to ₹2 lakh a year under Section 24(b); principal counts within 80C.

## Note
Most of these deductions are not available in the new regime. Limits can change in each Budget — verify on incometax.gov.in.
