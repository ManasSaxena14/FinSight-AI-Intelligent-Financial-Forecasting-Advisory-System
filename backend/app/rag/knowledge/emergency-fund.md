## How much
A common guideline is 3–6 months of essential expenses: rent, food, bills, EMIs, insurance premiums and school fees. Freelancers, single-income households and people in volatile jobs should aim for the higher end (6–12 months).

## Where to keep it
It must be safe and quickly reachable: a separate savings account, sweep-in FD, or liquid fund. Avoid equity for emergency money — markets often fall at the same time people lose income.

## How to build it
1. Calculate your essential monthly spend (FinSight's Rent + Food + Bills is a good start, plus any EMIs).
2. Multiply by your target months.
3. Automate a fixed transfer on salary day until you reach the target.
4. Refill it after you use it.

## Emergency fund vs goals
Build at least one month of buffer before starting long-term investments, and finish the full fund before taking on discretionary goals. A health-insurance policy works alongside the emergency fund, so a hospital bill does not wipe it out.
