## Fixed deposits (FDs)
An FD locks money for a fixed term at a fixed interest rate. Interest is taxed at your slab rate, and banks deduct TDS when interest crosses the yearly threshold. Breaking an FD early usually costs a small interest penalty. Senior citizens typically get a higher rate.

## Recurring deposits (RDs)
An RD lets you deposit a fixed amount every month for a set term — useful for short-term goals like a trip or a gadget within one or two years.

## Deposit insurance (DICGC)
Bank deposits — savings, current, FD and RD — are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, covering principal and interest together. Spreading large amounts across banks keeps more of your money insured.

## Sweep-in and liquid options for emergencies
Sweep-in FDs automatically move surplus savings into an FD and break it when you need cash, which suits an emergency fund. Liquid mutual funds are another option for quick access with low volatility, though they are not insured.

## Tax-saving FD
A 5-year tax-saving FD qualifies for 80C (old regime) but cannot be broken before maturity.
