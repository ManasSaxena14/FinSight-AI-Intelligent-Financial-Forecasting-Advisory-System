## What a SIP is
A Systematic Investment Plan invests a fixed amount in a mutual fund every month. It builds the habit, avoids trying to time the market, and averages your purchase price over time (rupee-cost averaging). You can start, pause, increase (step-up) or stop a SIP at any time.

## Index funds
An index fund simply copies a market index such as the Nifty 50 or Sensex. Costs (expense ratios) are low and there is no fund-manager risk. For many beginners, a low-cost broad-market index fund is a simple long-term core.

## ELSS (tax-saving funds)
Equity Linked Savings Schemes are equity mutual funds with a 3-year lock-in — the shortest among 80C options. Investments qualify for the ₹1.5 lakh 80C deduction under the old regime only. Each SIP instalment is locked for 3 years from its own date.

## Direct vs regular plans
Direct plans have lower expense ratios than regular plans because no distributor commission is paid. Over decades the difference compounds into a meaningful amount.

## Risk and time horizon
Equity funds can fall 20–40% in bad years. Money needed within 3 years usually belongs in safer options (savings, FDs, liquid or short-duration debt funds). Equity suits goals 5+ years away.
